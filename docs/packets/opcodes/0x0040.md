## S2C MotionBroadcast

```
+0x00  i8  count   SIGNED, loop bound is MOVSX, 0 or negative means no entries, max 127
 then  count x 25 byte entry
size   1+25*count on every ordinary track
```

Entry:

```
E+0x00  u32  player_id
E+0x04  i16  interp_scale_ms      0 divides by zero and NaNs the car, always send 1000
E+0x06  u64  packed_position      sub_44E7F0 decoder, 0.01 res, int part saturates at 4095
E+0x0E  u64  packed_target_pos    NOT a rotation, pos + dt*50*velocity
E+0x16  u8   yaw                  unit is 360/255 deg
E+0x17  u16  state_bits
```

ALT FORM when `sub_487230(dword_1ADF810)` is true, world id 20000000 only, stride 34:

```
E+0x00  u32       player_id
E+0x04  i16       interp_scale_ms
E+0x06  blob[0x1C] raw transform { f32 x,y,z, f32 tx,ty,tz, u8 yaw, u8 pad, u16 state_bits }
```

state_bits: b15..12 steer idx, b11..8 rpm bucket, b7 drift boost, b6 item boost,
b5 reverse, b4 drifting, b3 mini turbo charged, b2 lean left, b1 lean right, b0 unused.
Six reads per entry happen BEFORE the id lookup so an unknown id is stream safe.
Entry is consumed then DISCARDED when car+686272 is non zero, set by S2C 0x6A.
NEVER send a client its own player_id, the local car never reads this queue.
Queue depth is 1, latest wins.

## C2S SelfMotionReport

```
+0x00  u64  packed_position
+0x08  u64  packed_target_position
+0x10  u8   yaw
+0x11  u16  state_bits
size   19 default, 28 in the 20000000 raw world
```

NO player_id, NO count, NO interp_scale. Server attaches identity from the socket.
Fixed 100 ms send rate. Only sent while `dword_B2360C` is 9 or 11.

## Our server

### The server driven CPU cars

A bot has no client so `RaceHandler::tickBots` fills its entry itself. `packed_target_pos` must
carry what a real sender writes, `pos + frameDt * 50 * velocity`, because the receiver eases one
fiftieth of that gap per tick, so a lead of fifty frames is exactly the speed the car really has.
`BotDriver::lookahead` in `server/game/src/handlers/RaceBots.cpp` uses `speed * 50 / 60`, sixty
frames a second being what a stock client runs, and walks the racing line instead of casting a
straight ray so the anchor stays on the road through a corner. A shorter lead draws the remote
car a fixed distance behind where it really is, which is what made the CPU cars look like they
cut corners and slid through scenery.

The grid hold before GO publishes the same slot every tick with the car parked, otherwise
`MOTION_STALE_MS` 500 drops the CPU car from the fan out for the whole ten second hold.

`RaceHandler::handleMotion` in `server/game/src/handlers/RaceHandler.cpp`, routed from case
0x40 in `server/game/src/GameServerDispatch.cpp`, takes the 19 or 28 byte body while the room
is Racing, attaches the sender from the socket, then runs four judges before it relays the
sample to the other cars. Every strike goes into one leaky bucket, `suspiciousCount`, which
bleeds one point every `SUSPICION_DECAY_MS` 3000 and kicks past `SUSPICION_KICK` 10.

1. the motion gate, `MotionPackets::motionStep`, the per axis delta since the last sample
   against the client velocity clamp times the whole wire periods that passed times 1.25
2. `DriftBoostPackets::checkSpeed`, the distance speed against the kart ceiling, clamp of wire
   stat 1 plus 1 to 1..2 times 320 kmh in world units times 1.35, and a 300 unit single step
   teleport cap, a first sample or a gap over 3 s snaps and never accuses
3. `DriftBoostPackets::observeState`, the state word judge below
4. the standings and the relay

`observeState` in `server/game/src/packets/gen/DriftBoostPackets.cpp` reads the drift and boost
bits of every sample against the client machine of `docs/reverse/physics/TICK_HELPERS.md` (the
drift, settled) and the recorded cadence of `docs/reverse/physics/GHOST_REFERENCE.md` (the drift
recording). It counts samples, not clock ms, because the client sends at a fixed 100 ms period
and the receive clock jitters. What it accepts:

- bit 4 up starts a drift, bit 3 up inside it is stage 1, the charge, it cannot show before
  floor(hold / 100) samples with hold the wire 11 term `clamp(1 minus stat times 0.8, 0.2, 1)
  times 800 ms`, 352 ms and 3 samples on the basic kart, earlier is `drift_charge`
- bit 3 down with bit 4 still up is the key release, stage 2, the gauge decays at 120 a second
  so the state lives under 0.4 s more
- the gas edge fires stage 3 in the tick that clears the state, so a mini turbo is a class 0
  boost (bit 7) in the sample where bit 4 drops, one more sample of grace, off a drift that
  showed stage 1 or lasted longer than the hold. A class 1 boost (bit 6) in that spot is the 3
  percent lucky roll and passes too
- a class 0 boost anywhere else must sit on a kind 0 pad, a class 1 boost must pair with a C2S
  0x47 inside 600 ms or sit on a pad of another kind, else `miniturbo_fast` or
  `boost_unmatched`
- a class flip with no gap between two boosts is a pad or an item restart, a flip to class 0
  with neither is `boost_spam`
- bit 0 set is `reserved_bit`, bits 7 and 6 together `both_classes`, the encoder cannot write
  either

### The motion gate, rebuilt on the clamp

The old budget was `42 * 1.30 * 1.8 * 1.15`, about 113 world units a second, and every one of
those four numbers is marked INFERRED in `../PHYSICS_SPEED_VERIFIED.md`. Worse, it divided by a
`steady_clock` receive gap with a 50 ms floor, so two frames that arrive bunched inside one
client period got a 7.06 unit budget. A car on a straight moves 12.0 units in one period, so
the gate refused honest play, kept refusing because the baseline never advanced, and the strike
ladder reached the kick. Two test accounts were banned that way.

The numbers now come from the client integrator. `body_chassis_integrate_k1` clamps velocity x
and y at 120.0 (0x5a6a14) and z at 60.0 (0x5a6a68), and all three recordings under
`tools/replay/recordings` peak at 120.05 units a second on x and y, so 120 is a measured
ceiling and not a guess.

```
steps    = round(receive gap / 100 ms), at least 1, at most 10
budgetXY = 120 * steps * 0.1 * 1.25 = 15.0 per period
budgetZ  =  60 * steps * 0.1 * 1.25 =  7.5 per period
snap     = max(budget, 80)
```

The step count comes from the fixed 100 ms send period of `sub_495B20`, never from the receive
clock, the same rule `observeState` already followed. Bunched frames therefore still buy one
whole period.

A step over the budget but inside the snap reach is a rescue, not a cheat. 0x5a32a0 bounds the
respawn reprobe at 80 units and the largest rescue measured over the three recordings is 38.6
units on one axis in one sample. Those resync the remotes and add no strike. Only a step past
the snap reach accuses. The sample is always taken as the new truth, position is client
authority, so the gate can never wedge a car at a stale pose the way the old one did.

`checkSpeed` now quantises its own dt the same way, so a bunched pair no longer reads as ten
times the speed and no longer trips the ceiling either. Its 300 unit teleport cap and the kart
ceiling stay as they were, and they are what still catch a sustained speed hack: 79 units every
period is 790 units a second, far over the 380 the basic kart allows.

`tests/server/test_motion_gate.cpp` replays the three recordings through the gate and expects
zero accusations, and replays the old numbers over the same file to show they accuse it.

The pads are the `BOOST_NNN` cells of the track COL and its overlay pieces, read by
`SpawnPackets::loadTrackCheckpoints` with the kind of line NNN of `boost.ini` (kind 0 mini turbo
class, every other kind item class, kind 6 cancels and starts nothing). The test is a point in
the cell widened by 4 units for the wheel offset, swept over the straight path since the previous
sample, the client starts the boost on the wheel touch and the next sample sits up to a car
length past the cell. With no COL the observer never accuses a boost, only the drift charge and
the encoder bits stay live.

The kart numbers come from `RaceHandler::loadTuning`, the same 17 wire floats the login burst put
in the 0xC0 record (`kartWireStats`), plus the part overlay on a factory car, never the
`kart_catalog` seed alone, that table still holds the old meaning of its columns.

`tests/server/test_drift_boost_observer.cpp` replays
`tools/replay/recordings/drift_t90_c7_20260915.ghost`, the stock client race of 2026-09-15 on
Race 01 with 37 drifts, through `observeState` and `checkSpeed` with the pads of Race 01 and the
tuning of template 10010 and expects zero violations, 12 mini turbos, 20 pad boosts and one item
boost. The same file drives a boost with no drift and no pad, a mini turbo every second, a charge
on the first drift sample and a speed over the cap through the judges and expects each flagged.

### A sample in the same ms

`DriftBoostPackets::checkSpeed` answers `Rewind` when a sample carries the same server ms as the
last one. TCP keeps the order, so that is never a sample out of order: two reports rode one read,
which the docker port relay of a busy host does (gate run of 2026-09-23 on a host at 88 percent
CPU, while our client sent no two reports closer than 100 ms). `RaceHandler::handleMotion` drops the
newer one as before, the next period covers it, and logs it at debug as "bunched in one read"
instead of the old warning "out of order". It needs the next image.

### Our client

The stock sender `motion_send_0x40` (0x49BEB0) runs from the local car tick and sends when the real
clock `time_now_ms` has passed the period of `0x495B20` (100 ms) since the last send, then the
clock restarts at now: at most one report a frame, never two after a stall. Our `RaceScreen::tickWire`
and `RoomScreen::driveRoom` used the frame clock, a sum of capped frame spans read at the frame
start, so a slow frame before the send point put two reports 5 to 67 ms apart on the wire
(gate run of 2026-09-23, `race_items` wire log). Both now read the real clock at the send point,
and the game socket sets TCP NODELAY as the stock `sub_4769C0` does, so a report never waits for the
next one. A report gap over 500 ms is then a frame over 500 ms, the client prints those with
`[time] frame` and the parts of the frame.
